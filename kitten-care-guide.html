Kitten Care Guide: Feeding, Litter Training and Socialisation

A first-month guide to kitten nutrition, safe handling, litter habits, play and veterinary visits.

PawCare Guide · Updated November 2026

Kittens need a safe environment, complete nutrition, gentle handling and early veterinary care. Arrange an initial veterinary visit soon after adoption.

Prepare a safe space

Start with a quiet room containing food, water, litter, a bed, hiding place, scratching surface and safe toys.

Choose age-appropriate food

Feed complete and balanced kitten food suitable for growth. Kittens often need several small meals daily. Fresh water should always be available.

Make litter learning easy

Use a low-sided box in a quiet location. Repeated straining, crying or inability to pass urine needs urgent veterinary care.

Socialise through choice

Let the kitten approach at its own pace. Pair handling, carriers and new sounds with positive experiences.

Important: This article is general educational information, not a diagnosis or treatment plan. Contact a licensed veterinarian for advice about an individual pet. For breathing difficulty, collapse, seizures, suspected poisoning or inability to urinate, seek urgent veterinary care.

Frequently asked questions

When should a kitten see a vet?

Arrange an initial examination soon after bringing the kitten home and follow the clinic’s schedule.

Can kittens eat adult food?

Growing kittens generally need food formulated for growth unless a veterinarian advises otherwise.
