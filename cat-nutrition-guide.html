Cat Nutrition Guide: Protein, Water and Portion Control

Understand feline nutrition, complete food labels, hydration, meal routines, treats and body condition.

PawCare Guide · Updated November 2026

Cats are obligate carnivores with specific nutrient requirements. Choose food formulated for cats and the correct life stage, and ask a veterinarian about special dietary needs.

Choose complete cat food

Look for a complete and balanced food intended for the cat’s life stage. Supplemental foods are not designed to be the sole diet.

Support hydration

Place fresh water in multiple quiet locations and clean bowls regularly. Wet food can contribute moisture, but individual needs vary.

Measure meals and treats

Use package directions as a starting point and adjust with veterinary advice. Include treats and extras in daily intake.

Avoid unsafe diet experiments

Do not feed raw or unbalanced homemade diets without professional formulation. Ask your veterinarian about medical diets.

Important: This article is general educational information, not a diagnosis or treatment plan. Contact a licensed veterinarian for advice about an individual pet. For breathing difficulty, collapse, seizures, suspected poisoning or inability to urinate, seek urgent veterinary care.

Frequently asked questions

Are cats able to live on an unformulated vegetarian diet?

Cats have essential nutrient requirements that make an unformulated vegetarian diet unsafe.

Is wet food better than dry?

Both can be suitable. The best choice depends on nutrition, hydration, health and preference.
