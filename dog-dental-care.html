Dog Dental Care: Brushing and Oral Health

Understand tooth brushing, dental warning signs, professional checks and safe oral care.

PawCare Guide · Updated November 2026

Dental disease can cause pain and affect eating and quality of life. Dogs may continue eating despite mouth discomfort, so regular checks matter.

Build a brushing habit gradually

Introduce a pet toothbrush and toothpaste slowly. Aim to brush the outer tooth surfaces and never use human toothpaste.

Watch for warning signs

Bad breath, red gums, drooling, pawing at the mouth, dropping food or facial swelling deserve veterinary assessment.

Discuss professional care

A veterinary examination can identify problems below the gumline. Professional cleaning may require anaesthesia for a safe, thorough assessment.

Choose chews carefully

Some products may help reduce plaque, but hard objects can fracture teeth. Ask your veterinary team about suitable options.

Important: This article is general educational information, not a diagnosis or treatment plan. Contact a licensed veterinarian for advice about an individual pet. For breathing difficulty, collapse, seizures, suspected poisoning or inability to urinate, seek urgent veterinary care.

Frequently asked questions

How often should I brush?

Daily brushing is a useful goal; a consistent routine several times a week can also help.

Can bad breath be normal?

Persistent bad breath can signal oral disease and should be checked.
