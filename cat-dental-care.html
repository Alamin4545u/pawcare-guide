Cat Dental Care: Signs of Dental Disease and Prevention

Learn cat tooth brushing basics, oral disease warning signs and veterinary dental examinations.

PawCare Guide · Updated November 2026

Dental disease is common in cats and can be difficult to spot. A cat may continue eating even when its mouth hurts. Regular veterinary oral examinations matter.

Recognise changes in the mouth

Bad breath, drooling, red gums, pawing at the face, dropping food or reduced grooming may indicate oral discomfort.

Introduce brushing slowly

Let your cat sniff the brush and pet toothpaste, then build gradually toward gentle brushing. Never use human toothpaste.

Understand professional care

Your veterinarian may recommend a dental procedure after examining the mouth. Anaesthesia can be needed to assess and treat disease safely.

Avoid unsafe tools

Do not scrape tartar with sharp tools or force the mouth open. Ask about suitable dental products and a low-stress plan.

Important: This article is general educational information, not a diagnosis or treatment plan. Contact a licensed veterinarian for advice about an individual pet. For breathing difficulty, collapse, seizures, suspected poisoning or inability to urinate, seek urgent veterinary care.

Frequently asked questions

Does dry food clean teeth?

Ordinary dry food is not a substitute for dental care. Some specially designed diets may help.

What if my cat will not tolerate brushing?

Ask your veterinary team about other evidence-informed options.
