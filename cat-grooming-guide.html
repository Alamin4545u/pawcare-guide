Cat Grooming Guide: Brushing, Claws, Ears and Coat Care

Build a low-stress grooming routine with brushing, nail care, coat checks and professional help.

PawCare Guide · Updated November 2026

Cats groom themselves, but many benefit from brushing and regular checks. Keep sessions gentle and let your cat take breaks.

Brush according to coat length

Short-haired cats may need weekly brushing, while long-haired cats often need more frequent attention. Never cut a tight mat close to the skin.

Make nail care gradual

Introduce paw handling with rewards, then trim only the sharp tip. Ask a professional to demonstrate if needed.

Check ears, eyes and skin

Look for discharge, odour, redness, swelling or bald patches. Use ear products only when recommended by your veterinarian.

Get help for mats or distress

Severe mats can cause pain. A professional groomer or veterinarian can advise on safe removal.

Important: This article is general educational information, not a diagnosis or treatment plan. Contact a licensed veterinarian for advice about an individual pet. For breathing difficulty, collapse, seizures, suspected poisoning or inability to urinate, seek urgent veterinary care.

Frequently asked questions

Do indoor cats need grooming?

Some do. Coat length, age, weight and ability to groom themselves affect how much help they need.

Can I use human shampoo?

Use products recommended for cats; human shampoos may irritate their skin.
