Cat Litter Box Guide: Setup, Cleaning and Common Problems

Learn litter box placement, number, substrate, cleaning routines and when toileting changes need veterinary attention.

PawCare Guide · Updated November 2026

Litter box problems can have medical, environmental or social causes. Sudden changes in urination or defecation should not be treated as misbehaviour without checking for health problems.

Provide enough accessible boxes

A common guideline is one box per cat plus one extra. Place boxes in quiet accessible areas and avoid noisy appliances.

Choose a comfortable box

Many cats prefer a large uncovered box and unscented litter, though preferences differ. Older cats may need low-entry boxes.

Clean consistently

Scoop daily and wash periodically with mild unscented soap. Avoid harsh fragrances.

Treat sudden changes seriously

Frequent trips, straining, crying, blood or little/no urine require prompt veterinary attention. Inability to urinate is an emergency.

Important: This article is general educational information, not a diagnosis or treatment plan. Contact a licensed veterinarian for advice about an individual pet. For breathing difficulty, collapse, seizures, suspected poisoning or inability to urinate, seek urgent veterinary care.

Frequently asked questions

Why does my cat avoid the box?

Possible causes include illness, pain, location, litter preference, stress or conflict. A veterinary check is important.

Can boxes be side by side?

Separate locations are usually more useful because one stressful area can block access to all boxes.
