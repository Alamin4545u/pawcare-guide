Dog Food Safety: Toxic Foods and Kitchen Hazards

A dog food safety reference covering toxic foods, safe storage, choking hazards and accidental ingestion.

PawCare Guide · Updated November 2026

Many everyday foods and household products are unsafe for dogs. Risk depends on the substance, amount, dog size and timing. Contact a veterinarian or animal poison service promptly after suspected ingestion.

Keep high-risk foods away

Chocolate, grapes and raisins, xylitol, onions, garlic, alcohol, caffeine and macadamia nuts can cause serious problems.

Prevent access to rubbish

Secure bins, wrappers, medicines, cleaners and compost. Cooked bones and skewers can cause choking or digestive injury.

Store pet food safely

Keep food sealed, follow storage instructions, wash bowls and avoid spoiled or mouldy food.

Act quickly after ingestion

Note what was eaten, the amount and time. Keep packaging. Do not induce vomiting or give home remedies unless instructed by a veterinary professional.

Important: This article is general educational information, not a diagnosis or treatment plan. Contact a licensed veterinarian for advice about an individual pet. For breathing difficulty, collapse, seizures, suspected poisoning or inability to urinate, seek urgent veterinary care.

Frequently asked questions

Should I wait for symptoms after chocolate ingestion?

No. Contact a veterinarian or poison service promptly, even before symptoms appear.

Can I make my dog vomit at home?

Do not do so unless specifically directed by a veterinary professional.
