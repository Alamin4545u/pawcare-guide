Senior Dog Care: Comfort, Mobility and Health

Support older dogs with mobility, nutrition, home adjustments, veterinary checks and quality-of-life monitoring.

PawCare Guide · Updated November 2026

Ageing can bring changes in mobility, senses, appetite and organ function. Small adjustments and timely veterinary advice can help an older dog remain comfortable and engaged.

Notice changes early

Track weight, appetite, thirst, sleep, walking and behaviour. Difficulty rising, reluctance to use stairs, confusion, coughing or new accidents deserve veterinary attention.

Make the home easier to navigate

Use non-slip rugs, accessible beds, ramps where suitable and easy-to-reach food and water. Keep furniture arrangements predictable.

Review food, weight and activity

Ask your veterinarian whether food and calorie intake remain appropriate. Maintain gentle movement with rest breaks and avoid sudden increases in exercise.

Plan preventive visits

Older dogs may need more frequent examinations and age-appropriate screening. Discuss pain, dental health, vision, hearing and parasite prevention.

Important: This article is general educational information, not a diagnosis or treatment plan. Contact a licensed veterinarian for advice about an individual pet. For breathing difficulty, collapse, seizures, suspected poisoning or inability to urinate, seek urgent veterinary care.

Frequently asked questions

Should older dogs stop exercising?

Usually not. Activity should be adjusted to comfort and health with veterinary guidance.

Are behaviour changes just old age?

New behaviour changes can reflect pain, sensory loss or illness and should be discussed with a veterinarian.
