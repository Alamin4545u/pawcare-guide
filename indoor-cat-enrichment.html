Indoor Cat Enrichment: Play, Climbing and Mental Stimulation

Create a stimulating indoor environment with climbing spaces, scratching posts, hiding places and puzzle feeding.

PawCare Guide · Updated November 2026

Indoor cats benefit from opportunities to climb, scratch, stalk, hide and explore. Enrichment can be simple and inexpensive when resources are placed thoughtfully.

Create vertical and hiding spaces

Cat trees, stable shelves, window perches, boxes and covered beds give cats choices. Ensure structures are secure.

Schedule interactive play

Use wand toys to imitate prey movement and allow the cat to catch the toy. Store string toys safely after play.

Use food as enrichment

Puzzle feeders and scatter feeding encourage searching. Start with easy puzzles and ensure every cat gets enough food.

Reduce conflict in multi-cat homes

Provide separated food, water, litter, resting and scratching locations, with escape routes for each cat.

Important: This article is general educational information, not a diagnosis or treatment plan. Contact a licensed veterinarian for advice about an individual pet. For breathing difficulty, collapse, seizures, suspected poisoning or inability to urinate, seek urgent veterinary care.

Frequently asked questions

Do indoor cats get bored?

Some do, particularly without play, exploration or choice.

How often should I play?

Offer regular short sessions and adjust to your cat’s age, health and interest.
