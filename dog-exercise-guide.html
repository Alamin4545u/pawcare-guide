Dog Exercise Guide: Safe Activity for Every Stage

Plan walks, play, sniffing and enrichment around age, fitness, breed, weather and health.

PawCare Guide · Updated November 2026

Exercise supports physical fitness and mental wellbeing, but the right amount differs between dogs. Build activity gradually and watch for fatigue or discomfort.

Mix different activities

Walks, sniffing, play, safe swimming, food puzzles and training provide different forms of enrichment.

Adjust for age

Puppies need play and rest, not forced endurance exercise. Seniors may benefit from shorter, more frequent walks.

Watch weather and surfaces

Avoid intense activity in heat or humidity. Provide water and shade and check pavement temperature.

Increase activity gradually

Stop if you notice limping, excessive panting, weakness or reluctance to continue. Seek veterinary advice for concerning symptoms.

Important: This article is general educational information, not a diagnosis or treatment plan. Contact a licensed veterinarian for advice about an individual pet. For breathing difficulty, collapse, seizures, suspected poisoning or inability to urinate, seek urgent veterinary care.

Frequently asked questions

Does every dog need the same exercise?

No. Individual needs vary substantially.

Is mental enrichment useful?

Yes. Sniffing, training and puzzle activities provide stimulation alongside physical activity.
