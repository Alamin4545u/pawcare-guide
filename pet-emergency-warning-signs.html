Pet Emergency Warning Signs: When to Seek Veterinary Help

Recognise urgent warning signs in dogs and cats, prepare for an emergency and know what to tell a veterinary clinic.

PawCare Guide · Updated November 2026

Some health problems need immediate veterinary attention. When in doubt, call an emergency veterinary clinic and describe what you see. This guide must never delay urgent care.

Know urgent warning signs

Difficulty breathing, collapse, seizures, severe bleeding, major trauma, suspected poisoning or inability to stand are emergencies. A swollen abdomen with repeated unproductive retching in a dog is also urgent.

Watch urinary signs in cats

Repeated litter box trips, straining, crying or passing little/no urine can indicate a life-threatening blockage, especially in male cats. Seek emergency care immediately.

Call ahead and transport safely

Phone the clinic when possible. Keep an injured pet calm, minimise handling and use a carrier or secure support. Do not give human medication.

Prepare useful information

Bring medication details, records, product packaging and the time symptoms began. For possible poisoning, note the substance and estimated amount.

Important: This article is general educational information, not a diagnosis or treatment plan. Contact a licensed veterinarian for advice about an individual pet. For breathing difficulty, collapse, seizures, suspected poisoning or inability to urinate, seek urgent veterinary care.

Frequently asked questions

Should I wait if severe symptoms improve?

No. Severe breathing difficulty, collapse, seizures, major bleeding or suspected poisoning require urgent professional advice.

Can I use human painkillers?

Do not give human pain medicines unless a veterinarian specifically directs you.
