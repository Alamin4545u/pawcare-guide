Flea, Tick and Worm Prevention for Dogs and Cats

A general guide to parasite prevention, product safety, routine checks and local risks.

PawCare Guide · Updated November 2026

Fleas, ticks, heartworms and intestinal parasites affect pets differently depending on species, lifestyle, climate and location. Prevention should be selected for the individual animal and used exactly as labelled.

Ask about local risks

Your veterinarian can recommend prevention based on location, travel, outdoor access and contact with other animals.

Use species-specific products

Never use a dog parasite product on a cat unless the label explicitly says it is safe for cats. Some dog products containing permethrin are highly toxic to cats.

Keep a consistent schedule

Follow the label and veterinary instructions. Record application dates and do not combine or repeat doses early without advice.

Check pets and the home

Inspect skin after outdoor activity, wash bedding and follow veterinary advice for treating all pets if an infestation occurs.

Important: This article is general educational information, not a diagnosis or treatment plan. Contact a licensed veterinarian for advice about an individual pet. For breathing difficulty, collapse, seizures, suspected poisoning or inability to urinate, seek urgent veterinary care.

Frequently asked questions

Can I use the same flea product for dogs and cats?

Not automatically. Products are species-specific and some dog products are dangerous to cats.

Do indoor pets need prevention?

Some indoor pets still have exposure risks. Ask your veterinarian to assess your household.
