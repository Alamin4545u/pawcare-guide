Complete Cat Care Guide for New and Experienced Owners

A practical cat care guide covering food, water, litter, enrichment, grooming, health and home safety.

PawCare Guide · Updated November 2026

Cats need more than food and a litter box. A healthy environment supports climbing, hiding, scratching, play, rest and choice about social interaction.

Create a cat-friendly home

Provide safe resting and hiding places, vertical spaces, scratching surfaces and separate resources for food, water and toileting.

Feed a complete feline diet

Cats are obligate carnivores and need food formulated for their life stage. Keep fresh water available and ask your veterinarian about portions.

Support litter box comfort

A common guideline is one litter box per cat plus one extra. Place boxes in accessible quiet locations and scoop daily.

Use play and preventive care

Offer interactive play, puzzle feeders and rotating toys. Schedule examinations, vaccinations, dental care and parasite prevention.

Important: This article is general educational information, not a diagnosis or treatment plan. Contact a licensed veterinarian for advice about an individual pet. For breathing difficulty, collapse, seizures, suspected poisoning or inability to urinate, seek urgent veterinary care.

Frequently asked questions

Should cats live indoors?

Indoor living can reduce some risks, but cats still need enrichment and safe opportunities to explore.

How can I tell if my cat is unwell?

Watch for changes in eating, drinking, weight, grooming, hiding, movement and litter habits.
