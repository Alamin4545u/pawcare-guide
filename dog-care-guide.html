Complete Dog Care Guide for Beginners

Daily routines, feeding, grooming, exercise, training and preventive veterinary care for dogs.

PawCare Guide · Updated November 2026

Dog care combines nutrition, exercise, grooming, training, safety and preventive veterinary care. Needs vary with age, breed, body condition and health, so use this guide as a starting point and ask your veterinarian to personalise care.

Build a predictable routine

Set regular times for meals, toilet breaks, walks, play, training and rest. Keep fresh water available. Watch for changes in appetite, thirst, energy, stool or behaviour, as these can be early signs of illness.

Choose suitable food and portions

Select a complete and balanced food for your dog’s life stage. Measure portions, monitor body condition and include treats in the daily calorie total. Ask your veterinarian before making major dietary changes.

Make grooming and exercise consistent

Brush according to coat type, check ears and paws, and trim nails before they become uncomfortable. Combine walks, sniffing, play and enrichment, adjusting activity for fitness and weather.

Use reward-based training and preventive care

Reward desired behaviour with food, play or praise. Arrange routine veterinary examinations, vaccinations, dental checks and parasite prevention based on your dog’s individual needs.

Important: This article is general educational information, not a diagnosis or treatment plan. Contact a licensed veterinarian for advice about an individual pet. For breathing difficulty, collapse, seizures, suspected poisoning or inability to urinate, seek urgent veterinary care.

Frequently asked questions

How often should an adult dog see a veterinarian?

Many healthy adult dogs have at least an annual wellness visit. Puppies, seniors and dogs with ongoing conditions may need more frequent care.

Do all dogs need the same amount of exercise?

No. Breed, age, fitness, health and temperament all affect appropriate activity.
