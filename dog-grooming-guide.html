Dog Grooming Guide: Brushing, Bathing and Nails

Build a gentle grooming routine for coat care, bathing, nail trimming, ears, paws and dental hygiene.

PawCare Guide · Updated November 2026

Regular grooming helps you notice skin changes, mats, parasites and sore paws. The right routine depends on coat type, lifestyle and your dog’s tolerance.

Brush for the coat type

Short coats may need occasional brushing; long, curly or double coats often need more frequent care. Work gently and seek professional help for tight mats.

Bathe when needed

Use dog-specific shampoo and lukewarm water. Rinse thoroughly and dry well. Ask your veterinarian about persistent itching or flaking.

Trim nails gradually

Trim small amounts and avoid the quick. If nails are dark or your dog is anxious, ask a veterinary professional or groomer to demonstrate.

Check ears, paws and teeth

Look for redness, odour, discharge, swelling or cracked pads. Use pet toothpaste, never human toothpaste.

Important: This article is general educational information, not a diagnosis or treatment plan. Contact a licensed veterinarian for advice about an individual pet. For breathing difficulty, collapse, seizures, suspected poisoning or inability to urinate, seek urgent veterinary care.

Frequently asked questions

How often should a dog be bathed?

It depends on coat, skin and lifestyle; there is no single schedule for every dog.

What if my dog resists grooming?

Use gradual handling, short sessions and rewards. Seek help if fear or pain is involved.
