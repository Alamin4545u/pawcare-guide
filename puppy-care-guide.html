Puppy Care Guide: The First Year

Puppy feeding, safe socialisation, toilet training, sleep, vaccinations and positive training during the first year.

PawCare Guide · Updated November 2026

A puppy’s first year involves rapid physical and behavioural development. A safe home, suitable food, gentle socialisation and early veterinary care help establish healthy habits.

Prepare a safe home

Secure medicines, cleaners, cords, small objects and unsafe foods. Create a supervised area with a bed, water, safe chew toys and an identification tag.

Feed for growth

Choose complete and balanced puppy food. Large-breed puppies may need a diet formulated for controlled growth. Follow feeding guidance and monitor body condition.

Teach toilet habits calmly

Take your puppy out after waking, eating, drinking, playing and before bed. Praise immediately after success. Accidents are part of learning; clean them and avoid punishment.

Socialise and teach foundations

Introduce sounds, surfaces, people and safe dogs gradually. Ask your veterinarian how to balance socialisation with vaccine protection. Practise name response, recall and calm settling.

Important: This article is general educational information, not a diagnosis or treatment plan. Contact a licensed veterinarian for advice about an individual pet. For breathing difficulty, collapse, seizures, suspected poisoning or inability to urinate, seek urgent veterinary care.

Frequently asked questions

When can puppy training begin?

Reward-based foundation training can start when a puppy comes home, using short, positive sessions.

How much sleep does a puppy need?

Puppies need substantial sleep and quiet rest periods. Provide a calm, comfortable sleeping space.
