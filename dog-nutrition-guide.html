Dog Nutrition Guide: Choosing a Balanced Diet

Understand dog food labels, life-stage nutrition, portions, treats, water and diet changes.

PawCare Guide · Updated November 2026

Good nutrition supports growth, muscle, skin, coat and overall health. There is no single best food for every dog. Choose a complete and balanced product that suits the dog’s life stage and health needs.

Look for complete and balanced food

Check that the food is labelled as complete and balanced for the intended life stage under the standards used in your country.

Read labels thoughtfully

Consider the nutritional adequacy statement, feeding directions, manufacturer quality controls and access to qualified nutrition expertise. An ingredient list alone cannot establish quality.

Measure portions

Use a measuring cup or kitchen scale. Package feeding amounts are estimates; needs vary with age, activity, neuter status and health.

Change diets gradually

Unless advised otherwise by a veterinarian, transition over several days. Persistent vomiting, diarrhoea, itching or appetite changes need veterinary advice.

Important: This article is general educational information, not a diagnosis or treatment plan. Contact a licensed veterinarian for advice about an individual pet. For breathing difficulty, collapse, seizures, suspected poisoning or inability to urinate, seek urgent veterinary care.

Frequently asked questions

Is grain-free food always healthier?

No. Grain-free is not automatically better. Discuss a specific diet with your veterinarian.

Can dogs eat only homemade food?

A homemade diet needs professional formulation to avoid nutrient deficiencies.
